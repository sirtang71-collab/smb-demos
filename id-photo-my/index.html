🇲🇾 Malaysia ID Photo Maker

Forms get returned when your photo is the wrong size or the wrong file size. This tool crops your photo to the exact pixel size and lands the file inside the KB window your form asks for — Malaysian passport and MyKad, both 35×50 mm (413×591 px at 300 DPI).

🔒 100% processed on your device. No upload, no server, no tracking.

Sizes at 300 DPI: Passport 35×50 mm = 413×591 px · 100–1000 KB (portal) · MyKad 35×50 mm = 413×591 px · light-blue background. KB limits other than the passport portal window are editable defaults — always check the latest notice from the office you are applying to.

Malaysian passport — read first: if you apply inside Malaysia, do not bring a photo — Jabatan Imigresen Malaysia (JIM) captures your portrait on site (Facial Live Capture). A printed 35×50 mm photo is only specified for children under 4 and for overseas / mission applications. MyKad: JPN likewise takes the photo on site for new cards and renewals — this preset is for replacement / printed use where a photo is still asked for. Background: the current passport spec is plain white; blue is still the norm for MyKad, although JPN accepts white at most counters now.

1 · Choose your photo type (Pilih jenis foto)

2 · Background colour (Warna latar belakang)

This colour fills the background behind your photo (it shows through transparent areas of a PNG). This tool does not cut out or replace the background inside your photo — if the office wants a white background, take the photo against a white background first. Avoid wearing white against a white background, or blue against a blue one. Selecting a preset sets its usual background automatically (white for the passport, light blue for MyKad) — you can still change it here. The exact light-blue shade for MyKad is not specified by JPN; use the shade your counter asks for.

3 · Add your photo (Muat naik foto)

Your photo will appear here. Drag to move it.

4 · Export one photo in the right KB window (Eksport foto)

How the minimum KB is reached honestly (when the office sets one): first the JPEG quality is raised to the highest level that still fits the limit. If the file is still under the minimum, the tool adds standard JPEG padding data — the picture does not change at all, no blur, no noise — and the message will say so when padding was used. If too much padding would be needed (your original is too small), the tool refuses instead of handing you a file that would be rejected.

5 · Print sheet — several photos to cut out (Helaian cetakan)

Add a photo first — the sheet tiles the selected size onto one 10×15 cm page (1181×1772 px at 300 DPI), with dashed cut lines. Print at 100% scale (no fit-to-page).
